It's Not Magic: How AI Actually Works

🎙️ A story to start with

Press play, and two voices start talking — warm, funny, a little nerdy. A man and a woman, hosting what sounds exactly like a podcast: "How do you teach an old industrial giant to dance?"

Here's the thing: neither of them is real. No microphones. No people. A tool read a stack of boring documents and produced two AI voices chatting like old friends. The first time you hear it, it's hard not to feel a little split — amazed, and a little spooked. It felt like magic.

It isn't. By the end, you'll know exactly why. Let's pull back the curtain.

Computers used to be boringly predictable

For most of history, a computer was a predictable box. Inputs in, work happens, the same output comes out — every time. 2 + 2 is always 4. Water is water. That sameness has a name: deterministic.

Spreadsheets, calculators, the cash register — reliable, repeatable, and clearly not alive. Nobody thinks a calculator is magic.

What changed? Scale, not sorcery

The basic idea didn't change. What exploded is the scale of two things:

  • Data — a nearly endless ocean of what people have written online.
  • Compute — speed. What took a mainframe days now happens instantly.

That speed is why AI feels magical. And everything on your screen — including an AI's reply — is generated from code. AI writes code, fast, and shows you the result. It's not magic, it's code.

Making sentences isn't that mysterious

People are more predictable than we admit. If someone says "that's a load of bull——," your brain already knows the next word. There are only so many ways to arrange words into a sentence that makes sense (music's the same with notes).

That's basically what a chatbot does: a very fast machine for guessing what word probably comes next, over and over. Suddenly those two podcast voices look a lot less mysterious.

The twist: it won't give the same answer twice

Ask AI the same question twice and you'll usually get two different answers. It predicts a likely response, and "likely" wiggles each time. We call this non-deterministic.

So you can't just re-ask and expect the same thing. And you don't need the deep math to use it — like driving a car without knowing the engine. Keep this model: code + data + speed, and non-deterministic.

🎙️ Back to those two voices

No people, no mics. A tool read documents (data), used huge compute to predict natural sentences, and turned it into code that played as voices. Run it again, you'd get a different chat — because it's non-deterministic. Magic? Not even a little.

Remember:

  • It's not magic — it's code, from huge data + fast compute.
  • AI is non-deterministic: same prompt, different answers.
  • Speed makes it feel magical; the substance is ordinary code.
  • You don't need the math — a simple model is enough to use it wisely.
