# Activity — Catch AI Being "Not Magic"

**Your goal:** see with your own eyes that AI is a fast pattern-machine, not magic. You're not
trying to get a perfect answer — you're trying to *notice how it behaves.*

---

## Part 1 — The "same prompt, different answer" test

This is the one that proves AI is **non-deterministic.**

1. Open any chatbot.
2. Start a **fresh chat** and send exactly this:
   > *In two sentences, explain why the sky is blue.*
3. Do it again in **two more fresh chats** — same prompt, three times total.
4. Read all three answers side by side.

**Write down:** What stayed the same across the three answers? What was different?

```
Same: _______________________________________________________________________

Different: ___________________________________________________________________
```

> 💡 If the answers were word-for-word identical, you'd be using a calculator. They weren't —
> that difference *is* non-determinism.

---

## Part 2 — "Treat me like I'm five"

1. Ask the chatbot:
   > *Explain like I'm five — how do you come up with your answers?*
2. Read its explanation.

**Write down:** In your own words, how does it say it works? Did it mention "magic," or did it
mention something more like *patterns* and *guessing what comes next*?

## Part 3 — Predict the next word

No computer needed for this one — *you* are the prediction machine.

Finish each line with the first word that pops into your head:

- "That's a piece of ______."
- "Better late than ______."
- "It's raining cats and ______."

**Think about it:** You barely had to try, right? That "what comes next" feeling is basically
what a chatbot does — just across billions of examples, very fast. Making sentences isn't as
mysterious as it seems.

---

## Reflection (the one thing to take with you)

Finish this sentence:

> **"AI is like ____________________, not like ____________________."**

*(Example: "like a very fast pattern-matcher, not like a calculator that always gives the same
answer.")*

---

## Discussion questions

Bring your answers to these — there are no "gotcha" right answers, just good thinking:

1. If AI is "just code + data + speed," how should that change how much you trust its answers?
2. The same prompt can give different answers. When is that unpredictability a **good thing**,
   and when is it a **problem**?
3. AI is running low on human writing to learn from. What does that tell you about where its
   "knowledge" actually comes from?
4. "This isn't a math class." Why might a simple mental model matter *more* than the technical
   details for most people who use AI?
