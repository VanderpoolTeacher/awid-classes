# Instructor Guide — Organize Your Work

## Why this lesson exists
This is the unglamorous-but-essential lesson — the foundation templates and systems stand on.
Make it personal: have everyone picture their own Downloads folder. The hook is that the mess is
a *habit* problem AI can now help with, not a tools problem.

## Learning objectives
1. **Explain** why project folders make you and AI more effective. *(Understand)*
2. **Distinguish** a useful information architecture from a random pile. *(Analyze)*
3. **Apply** two habits — a project log and a sandbox. *(Apply)*
4. **Evaluate** your own setup and pick one improvement. *(Evaluate)*

## Materials & setup
- A chatbot on the projector, and a genuinely messy folder to use in the demo.
- The student **activity handout** printed or linked.

## Timing at a glance
| Segment | Time |
|---|---|

## Facilitation walk-through
### 1. Open with the graveyard
Have everyone open (or picture) their Downloads folder. Name the real cause: organizing is
tedious human work we skip — and AI can do the tedious part.

### 2. Teach the through-line
- **Habit, not tools** — we've always had folders.
- **Project folders** — one home per effort; focused folder = focused AI help.
- **Information architecture** — structure + naming; AI can design it.
- **Project log** — two lines a day; future-you's memory and honesty trail.
- **Sandbox** — break things safely; promote what works.
- **Foundation** — templates and systems depend on this.

### 3. Live demo
With a messy real folder, ask AI to propose a folder structure and naming convention. Show how
fast the "boring part" gets done.

### 4. Activity
Hand out the worksheet. The log + sandbox steps are the habits to leave with.

### 5. Discussion
Draw out where missing organization (or a missing log) has cost people before.

### Closing thought
"This is the least glamorous habit in the course — and quietly the one that makes templates,
systems, and everything else actually hold together."

## Check-for-understanding — answer key
1. A focused folder keeps a project's pieces together and gives AI a clear, contained context
   (instead of your whole scattered drive).
2. Information architecture is the structure and naming of folders/files; it makes things
   findable — a logical place for everything.
3. A short running note of what you did and why (and what you asked AI); for future-you and
   teammates, so the work stays understandable and trustworthy.
4. A no-stakes folder for experiments; keep experiments there so you don't break real, important
   work.
5. Any: proposing a folder structure/naming convention, sorting a messy folder, organizing notes.

## Common misconceptions to watch for
- "Organizing is busywork." (It's the foundation templates and systems depend on.)
- "I'll remember why I did it." (Future-you won't — the log is cheap insurance.)
- "More folders = more organized." (Structure and consistent naming matter, not folder count.)
