# Organize Your Work

## A story to start with

Open your Downloads folder. Be honest.

It's probably a graveyard — hundreds of files named things like `document(3)-FINAL-v2.pdf`,
screenshots from months ago, things you will never find again. We all have the pile.

Here's the frustrating part: the tools to stay organized have always existed. Folders aren't new.
The hard part was never the technology — it was *us.* Staying organized is tedious, so we skip
it, and the junk drawer grows.

That changes the moment you pair a little structure with AI, which is perfectly happy to do the
tedious part. This lesson sets up a simple home for your work so you — and your AI — can actually
find it, trust it, and build on it: **work in project folders, give them a sensible structure,
keep a short log of what you did, and keep a sandbox where you can break things safely.** It's
not fancy. It's the quiet habit that makes templates and systems actually hold together.

---

## The mess isn't a tools problem — it's a habit problem

We've always had folders. Digital life turns into a junk drawer not because we're missing some
app, but because organizing is boring work we put off. So the fix isn't more willpower. It's a
simple structure plus a helper — AI — that doesn't mind the boring part.

## Work in project folders

First habit: give each effort its own **project folder**, and keep everything for it in one
place — drafts, notes, final files. This matters double with AI. These tools work far better when
you point them at *one clear folder* than at your whole scattered drive. A focused folder gets
you focused help.

## Give it an information architecture

A pile of files in a single folder is still a pile. **Information architecture** is just the
*structure* — a logical place for each kind of thing, and consistent names so you can find them
later.

You don't have to design it alone. This is a great job for AI: "here's my mess; propose a folder
structure and a naming convention," then let it help sort things in. Decide the structure once,
reuse it everywhere.

## Keep a project log

Second habit: a short **log** of what you did and why. When you build with AI, jot down what you
asked for and what changed over time.

It sounds like overhead. It's the opposite. It's how future-you understands the work without
re-doing the thinking, how a teammate picks it up, and how you answer "wait, why did we do it
this way?" three months from now. (This very course was built with exactly that kind of running
log.)

## Keep a sandbox

Third habit: a **sandbox** folder — a no-stakes place to experiment, try a wild idea, or break
something on purpose. Don't run experiments inside your real, important work. Play in the
sandbox; when something works, move it into the real project. It's how you learn a tool fast
without risking anything that matters.

## Why this is the foundation

Organization isn't busywork — it's what makes the powerful stuff *possible.* Templates live in
folders. Repeatable systems run on organized inputs. AI gives better help when your material has
a shape. Get this base right and everything else stops being fragile.

---

## Wrapping up: back to the Downloads graveyard

Remember that graveyard of a folder?

You don't fix it with one heroic cleanup that lasts a week. You fix it with a little structure
and a helper: point AI at the mess, let it propose folders and names, keep your real work in
project folders, jot a two-line log, and play in a sandbox. The pile stops growing because the
*system* — not your willpower — keeps it in order.

It's the least glamorous habit in this whole course, and quietly the one that makes the rest
work.

**Remember:**
- The mess is a **habit** problem, not a tools problem — and AI can do the tedious part.
- **Work in project folders**; give them an **information architecture.**
- **Keep a log** (your memory + honesty trail) and a **sandbox** (safe experiments).
- Organization is the **foundation** templates and systems stand on.
