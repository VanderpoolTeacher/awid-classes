Build Systems, Not Outputs

🏭 A story to start with

Ask an AI to "build me a course." It hands you something. Ask again tomorrow — and you get something different. Different structure, style, order. Do it ten times and you've got ten one-off documents that don't match.

That's not a glitch — AI makes a fresh, likely answer each time, not the same one twice.

The shift: stop asking AI for the finished thing. Use it to build the system that produces the finished thing.

Why one-off requests drift

Ask for the whole finished thing and you're at the mercy of AI's unpredictability — fine for one quick task, a disaster when you need ten lessons, reports, or proposals that belong to the same family. They won't match.

Build the system

The fix isn't a cleverer prompt — it's to build the system that makes the outputs. A machine shop doesn't carve each part by hand; it builds the machines that stamp out consistent parts.

With AI, your "machines" are templates, rules, and organized inputs. You invest once in the system; then every output comes out of the same mold.

Templates: your single source of truth

Don't tell AI "make more like this document" — that drifts. Take a good document, strip the content down to a labeled placeholder skeleton, and save that as the template. Now it's the one source of truth.

When you find an improvement, push it back into the template — don't let a random output quietly become your new standard.

AI works best from organized information

Messy in, messy out. If your inputs are a scattered pile, so are your outputs.

No organized information yet? That's a perfect first job for AI — have it help organize what you've got, then build your system on the clean base. First time costs more; every time after is faster, more consistent, and easier to fix.

🏭 Back to "build me a course"

You don't fix ten mismatched courses with a better request — you fix it by building the system: a template that defines the shape, organized inputs to draw from, and a simple process to run them through. Ask once for the mold, not over and over for the part. The AI stays unpredictable; your results stop being.

Remember:

  • AI is non-deterministic; one-off requests drift.
  • Build the system, not the one-off output.
  • Templates are the source of truth — push improvements back in.
  • Organize first (with AI if needed), then build.
