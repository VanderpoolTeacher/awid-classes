# The Production Pipeline

## A story to start with

Picture a single sheet of paper. On it is a standard: "to be certified for this job, a learner
must be able to do these five things." That's the whole seed.

Now picture that one page becoming an *entire course* — an outline, a dozen lessons, instructor
guides, activities, slides, quizzes — all produced by AI, all reviewed by a human, and all
checked back against that original one page, every step of the way.

That's a **production pipeline**: a chain of small, repeatable steps where AI does the heavy
lifting and a human checks the work at each stage. It's the capstone of this whole module — what
you get when you combine *build the system, not the output*, organized work, and good tooling
into one assembly line. This lesson shows how to chain the steps, where the human has to stay in
the loop, and how checking against a source of truth keeps a fast machine honest.

---

## Chain small steps, not one giant prompt

You *could* ask AI to "make the whole course" in one shot. You'll get a mess — too much at once,
impossible to check, impossible to fix.

A **pipeline** does the opposite. It breaks the job into small stages: standard → course outline
→ lesson outlines → lesson content → guides, activities, slides, quizzes. Each stage is a focused
task AI does well — and a small thing *you* can actually review before it feeds the next stage.

## Keep a human in the loop

Here's the model that makes this safe: **treat AI like a fast, capable entry-level employee.** It
produces a lot, quickly — and it doesn't always follow the rules. So you don't let it ship
unsupervised. At each stage, a human reviews and signs off.

Your job shifts from *doing the work* to *reviewing the work* — which is exactly where your
expertise is worth the most.

## Check every stage against the source of truth

What keeps a fast pipeline honest is a **source of truth** — the standard, spec, or brief the
work must match. At each stage, check the output against it: did the outline cover all five
required skills? Does this lesson still serve the standard?

This matters because errors **compound** down a chain. A wrong outline poisons every lesson built
from it. Catching drift early is everything. You can even have AI run the check — review the
draft against the standard and flag mismatches — as long as a human confirms.

## Why this is the capstone

Look at what a pipeline pulls together: it's a **repeatable system** (not a one-off), it runs on
**organized inputs and templates**, and it's wrapped in **human review and verification.** That's
this entire module in one machine.

The payoff is real leverage: one person can produce what used to take a team — *and* keep it
consistent and checked — because the machine handles the volume and the human handles the
judgment.

## One caution

A pipeline makes you fast, which means it can make mistakes fast, too. The discipline is the
checkpoints. A pipeline with no human review isn't efficient — it's a confident error machine.
**Speed plus checkpoints** is the win. Speed alone is the trap.

---

## Wrapping up: back to the one page

Remember the single sheet of paper becoming a whole course?

That only works because of the shape: small stages instead of one giant ask, a human signing off
at each one, and every step checked against that original page so nothing drifts. Take any of
those away and it falls apart — too big to check, or fast but wrong.

That's the capstone of building with AI: not a magic prompt, but a well-built assembly line where
the machine does the volume and you do the judgment.

**Remember:**
- **Chain small steps**, not one giant prompt.
- **Keep a human in the loop** — your job is review and sign-off.
- **Check every stage against a source of truth** — errors compound, so catch drift early.
- A pipeline is the **capstone**: a repeatable system + organized inputs + human review = real,
  checked leverage.
