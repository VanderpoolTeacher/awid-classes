# Activity — Is This Fair? Judge It for Yourself

**Your goal:** practice *reasoning* about fairness and sourcing. There's no single right
answer — the point is to think it through and be able to defend your call.

---

## Part 1 — Style with a name vs. without

1. Ask an image generator for a picture **"in the style of [a famous living artist]."**
2. Ask it for the **same scene with no artist named.**
3. Compare the two results.

**Write down:** Which one would you feel okay publishing, and why? What changed when you removed
the name?

## Part 2 — Find a safe well

Find one **public-domain** source you could actually use without an ownership problem (try
searching *"NASA images"* or *"[your city] public records"* or *"public domain photos"*).

**Write down:** the source, and one thing you could make from it.

```
Source: ______________________________________________________________________

```

> 💡 Public-domain and your-own work are the wells you can always drink from safely.

## Reflection (the one thing to take with you)

Finish this sentence:

> **"Using AI feels fair to me when ____________________, and unfair when ____________________."**

---

## Discussion questions

1. Is using AI trained on artists' work without consent "theft," "fair game," or something in
   between? Where do *you* draw the line?
2. You can't copyright an idea, only a specific expression. Does that feel right for the AI era?
3. When — if ever — should you tell people you used AI? Who deserves to know?
4. "The tool isn't the ethics, you are." Where have you seen the same tool used both well and
   badly?
