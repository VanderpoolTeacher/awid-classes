Whose Work Is It?

🎨 A story to start with

Someone photographs a downtown art museum, runs it through an AI tool, and adds dramatic laser lights swirling around it. It looks amazing. They post it.

And there's a knot in their stomach — because some people in the arts community will see it and feel robbed. The AI learned from countless artists who never agreed, were never paid, were never credited. To them, that's not a fun effect. It feels like theft.

AI learns by soaking up human-made work. So: whose work is it, and is using AI fair?

The objection: "you used my work without asking"

Sit in a creator's chair. AI got good by training on mountains of human work — much of it copyrighted, almost none of it licensed. So generating art "in the style of" a real artist can feel like their life's work was taken to compete against them.

It's serious and widely held — and the people who feel it may be exactly the audience you're trying to reach. (We saw the same fight when music suddenly became free to download.)

What the law protects: ideas vs. expression

Surprise: you can't copyright an idea. "A detective story set in space" belongs to everyone. What's protected is your specific expression — the exact words, the exact image you made.

That cuts both ways: AI can riff on common ideas legally, and your specific, hard-won work is yours even though the general topic isn't.

Fair use is a gray area — prefer safe sources

"Fair use" sometimes allows limited use of protected work, but for AI it's unsettled — don't treat it as a free pass. Red flags: a tool reproducing a stock-photo watermark, or copying a living, named artist's exact style.

The safe path: prefer public-domain or owned sources. Government/NASA images are free to use; your own photos and writing are yours. Then the ownership question mostly disappears.

Keep an honest trail — and remember it's about intent

You don't have to label everything "made with AI," but keep a quiet note of how you used it. If anyone asks, that honesty trail protects you.

In the end, AI is a tool — a hammer builds a house or breaks a window. The tool isn't the ethics; you are. Organizing your own notes is a world away from passing off an artist's style as your own.

🎨 Back to the laser lights

Nothing about the tool decided whether that post was okay — the person did. Fun lasers on your own photo for a casual crowd? Fine. Imitating a specific artist's look and selling it as original to people who'd feel cheated? Different entirely. Same image, same tool — the fairness lives in the choices around it.

Remember:

  • Many creators see AI as trained on unlicensed work — a real objection.
  • Copyright protects expression, not ideas.
  • Fair use is a gray area — prefer public-domain or owned sources.
  • Keep an attribution trail; the ethics live in your intent.
