Who Wins With AI?

🖥️ A story to start with

The first computers were built to do one thing: calculate where battleship shells would land. Room-sized, expensive, run by specialists.

Then businesses got them — and here's the surprise. The people who got the most out of them weren't the engineers who built them. They were the accountants, salespeople, and clerks — people who knew a job and used the new tool to do it faster.

That pattern is repeating with AI. The worry "I'm not technical, so AI isn't for me" gets it backwards.

The tool doesn't supply the judgment

AI is a tool. A hammer is dangerous in careless hands and powerful in skilled ones — the difference is the person. AI will confidently do whatever you point it at, well or badly.

So the real question isn't "what can AI do?" It's "what do you bring to it?"

You bring expertise + intent

The winners with AI usually know a field deeply and know what they want — not the ones with the most technical skill. A great teacher pulls more useful help out of AI for a lesson than a brilliant programmer who's never taught.

Just like the accountants out-using the engineers: your real-world expertise is the scarce ingredient. AI supplies speed; you supply the judgment.

Get closer to the controls

Tools hide their inner workings so they're easy to use — that's abstraction (why you can drive without understanding the engine). But you usually get whatever the default hands you.

Steer it: be specific ("cut to 100 words for a sixth-grader, keep the three points") and ask for structure (a list, a table) when it matters. You don't need to be technical — you need to be clear.

Agents — and knowing your edge

AI increasingly doesn't just answer — it can act. An agent takes steps for you (browse, fill a form, complete a task); a good one states its plan and pauses for your approval before anything important.

And know where your expertise ends. A confident non-expert plus a confident AI is how bad decisions happen fast — for high-stakes or out-of-field work, bring in a real human expert.

🖥️ Back to the accountants

The accountants got more out of the first computers than the engineers did — because they knew the job. That's you. You don't need to build the tool or understand its guts. You need to know your field, know what you want, and steer clearly — then check the work against what you know.

Remember:

  • AI is a tool; the value is in the hands holding it.
  • A domain expert who's clear about the goal beats a technical novice.
  • Get closer to the controls — be specific, ask for structure.
  • Know the edge of your expertise; high-stakes work needs a human expert.
