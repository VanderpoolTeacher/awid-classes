Your Data Isn't Private

📋 A story to start with

It's late, the report's due, and you've got a messy spreadsheet — customer names, emails, notes. You copy it, paste it into a free chatbot, and type "clean this up and summarize." Ten seconds later — beautiful. Done.

And in those ten seconds, you may have handed hundreds of people's private information to a company you don't control — stored, maybe used to train future models, possibly kept forever. You can't pull it back.

The most dangerous click in AI isn't a download. It's paste.

Nothing you put online is truly private

Anything you put on the internet may not be private, and it tends not to disappear. These systems copy, cache, log, and back up data — that's how they work.

A simple way to say it: everything you do on a computer can be tracked, and once it's out there, you can't fully take it back. Public AI tools are part of that same internet.

Be deliberate — and use the placeholder trick

Before you paste, ask one question: is this mine to share? If it has other people's names, emails, health or money details — strip it, anonymize it, or don't paste it.

The placeholder trick: swap real names and numbers for stand-ins ("Customer A," "$X"), get your summary, then restore the real details yourself, locally. The AI helps — but never sees the private parts.

Some data is protected by law; free tools want your data

In many fields it's the law: health info is protected by HIPAA, student records by FERPA. If you work in a regulated space, know the rules before you point AI at the data.

And free tools usually reserve the right to use your data — often to train future models. Read the terms. On a free personal account, assume anything you type could be used and kept.

At work, governance comes first

In a business, the data isn't yours — it's the company's, and competitors must never get it. Organizations set up governance: rules for what data exists, who can see it, and which tools may touch it.

So the question isn't only "can AI do this?" It's "is this data cleared for this tool?"

📋 Back to that paste

The tool did its job. The problem was the decision to paste private data into a place the user didn't control. One small habit fixes it: swap the names for placeholders first, or check whether the data was cleared for that tool. Same great summary — nobody's information leaves the building.

Remember:

  • Anything online may not be private — and it doesn't disappear.
  • Keep personal/regulated/confidential data out; anonymize when you can.
  • Free usually means your data is fair game — read the terms.
  • At work, governance decides what's allowed.
