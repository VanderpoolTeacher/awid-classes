# Instructor Guide — Your Data Isn't Private

## Why this lesson exists
This is the protect-yourself-and-others lesson. The hook is how *easy* and *irreversible* a bad
paste is. Open with the customer-list scenario and let "you can't take it back" land before
teaching the habits. The goal is confident, safe use — not fear.

## Learning objectives
1. **Explain** why data put into a public AI tool may not stay private. *(Understand)*
2. **Distinguish** safe-to-share data from data to withhold (personal/regulated/confidential).
   *(Analyze)*
3. **Apply** safe-data habits — anonymize, prefer scoped tools, check the terms. *(Apply)*
4. **Evaluate** whether a given AI use meets privacy/regulatory obligations. *(Evaluate)*

## Materials & setup
- A chatbot on the projector for the placeholder-trick demo.
- One AI tool's terms/privacy page open to show real language.
- The student **activity handout** printed or linked.

## Facilitation walk-through
### 1. Open with the scenario
Walk through pasting a customer list into a free chatbot for a quick summary. Pause on the
irreversibility: it may be stored, used to train, kept forever. *The riskiest click is paste.*

### 2. Teach the through-line
- **Nothing online is truly private** — tracked, logged, persistent.
- **Be deliberate** — "is this mine to share?"; anonymize.
- **Regulated data** — HIPAA/FERPA; know the rules first.
- **Free vs. paid** — free usually means your data is fair game; read the terms.
- **Governance first (at work)** — "is this data cleared for this tool?"

### 3. Live demo — the placeholder trick
Paste an **anonymized** snippet ("Customer A," "$X") and show the summary is just as useful
without any real names. This is the habit you want them to leave with.

### 4. Activity
Hand out the worksheet. Make sure learners actually open a real **terms page** in Part 2 — seeing
the language is the lesson.

### 5. Discussion
Surface the trade-off honestly: free tools are paid for by *something*. Keep it practical.

### Closing thought
"AI is great for the work. Just be the person who decides — before the paste — what's safe to
share."

## Check-for-understanding — answer key
1. Public tools store/log/back up what you submit and may use it (e.g., to train models), and
   online data persists — you can't reliably take a paste back.
2. Any two: names/contact info, health or financial details, IDs, other people's confidential
   data.
3. Replace real names/numbers with placeholders before pasting, get the result, then restore the
   real details locally — the AI never sees the private parts.
4. Free tiers usually reserve the right to use/keep your data (often to train models), so
   sensitive data is exposed.
5. Health data — HIPAA; student records — FERPA (either is acceptable).

## Common misconceptions to watch for
- "I can delete it, so it's fine." (Deletion doesn't undo exposure.)
- "It's just a summary, the data doesn't matter." (The *input* is the exposure.)
- "Privacy rules don't apply to little me." (HIPAA/FERPA/company policy apply regardless.)

## If you have less time
Keep the opening scenario, the placeholder-trick demo, and Part 1 of the activity. Those carry
the lesson.
