# Activity — Catch AI Being Confidently Wrong

**Your goal:** see AI hallucinate, learn to spot the "tells," and practice the verification
habit. You're not trying to get a clean answer — the point is to *check.*

---

## Part 1 — Hunt for fake sources

This is the one that proves **hallucination** is real.

1. Pick a topic you know a little about. Ask a chatbot:
   > *Give me three sources (with author and title) about [your topic].*
2. Now **try to find each source** online — search the title and author.

**Write down:** how many of the three were real, and how many you could not find?

```
Real: ____    Couldn't find / made up: ____
```

> 💡 Any source you can't find independently should be treated as fabricated until proven
> otherwise.

## Part 2 — Check a fact

1. Ask the chatbot a specific factual question with a checkable answer (a date, a population, a
   famous quote).
2. **Verify it** against a trusted website.

**Write down:** was it right, close, or wrong?

## Part 3 — Make AI check itself (then check it anyway)

1. Paste something the chatbot wrote and ask:
   > *List anything in here that might be fabricated or that you can't verify.*
2. Read its list. Then pick one item and **verify it yourself.**

**Write down:** did its self-check catch everything, miss something, or flag something that was
actually fine?


## Reflection (the one thing to take with you)

Finish this sentence:

> **"I will verify hard when ____________________, and I can relax when ____________________."**

---

## Discussion questions

1. Why is AI often *most* convincing when it's wrong? What does that do to how carefully you
   should read?
2. Where in your own work would a confident-but-false answer be most costly? How would you guard
   against it?
3. Bias is quieter than a fake fact. How might it slip past you — and who could it harm?
4. How do you personally decide what counts as "high stakes"?
